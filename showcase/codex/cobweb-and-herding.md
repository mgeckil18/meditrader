# Cobweb and herding

**Literature.** When there is a lag between decision and outcome (sowing and harvest, loading and arrival), producers who react to the last price overshoot, and the price oscillates (Kaldor 1934; Ezekiel 1938). Herding: agents follow the same signal or each other (Scharfstein & Stein 1990; Banerjee 1992; Kirman 1993).

**Mechanism in the model.** Merchants decide on prices they heard days or weeks ago. Nothing tells them to coordinate or to avoid each other.

**What appeared.**
- With "last observation" expectations, merchants who heard the same old "Istanbul is expensive" loaded at once. The cargo arrived together and the price collapsed. With 40 merchants the price gap fell below the transport cost (0.4-0.9x) and bankruptcies rose. [Experiment 02](../experiments/02-information-delay.md).
- Expectations that widen with age and revert to the usual price damped it: arrival irregularity 0.91 → 0.73.
- Rumours of who loaded what for where decided whether a multi-good world collapsed into a herd. Without them, merchants ended up waiting to sell pepper in one port and prices exploded in 4 of 5 seeds; with them, in none. [D5b](../../docs/experiments/D5b.md) (Turkish).

**Player text.**
> When there is time between a decision and its result, everyone acts on the same old signal. Merchants race to the same port like a herd; the first to arrive sells well, the rest find the price already fallen. Before you follow the crowd, ask how many ships have already sailed.
