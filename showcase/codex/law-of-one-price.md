# Law of one price

**Literature.** In a competitive market, the price of a good in two places differs by at most the cost of moving it between them (Cournot 1838; Samuelson 1952's spatial price equilibrium; Takayama & Judge 1971).

**Mechanism in the model.** Nothing enforces it. Ports have their own markets; merchants carry goods when the expected margin after freight, customs and risk is positive.

**What appeared.** With instant information and 15 greedy merchants, the Alexandria-Istanbul wheat gap settled at 1.07x the transport cost. Without trade it was 12.7x. Istanbul's shortage days fell from 55% to 6%. [Experiment 01](../experiments/01-instant-information.md).

**Player text.**
> If the price gap between two ports is larger than the cost of freight, customs and risk, someone will carry the goods and close it. If it is smaller, nobody will. A gap that stays open is telling you something: either nobody knows about it yet, or the risk is larger than it looks.
